Dear Editor,

Please find enclosed our manuscript entitled “Rapid quenching by ram-pressure
stripping in a young cluster at $z = 1.7$”, which we submit for your
consideration in Nature Astronomy.

Ram-pressure stripping is one of the few quenching channels that can be caught
in the act, but almost every resolved example is a nearby one. The system
reported here is the first in which the stripping radius, the surviving
molecular reservoir and the kinematic asymmetry can all be measured in the same
object at $z > 1$ — the epoch at which the quenched populations these clusters
now contain were actually assembled. That combination is what lets us say the
gas is being removed from the outside in, rather than inferring it from the
integrated colours of a stacked sample.

We believe this is a result for Nature Astronomy rather than a specialist
venue because it bears on the general question of how environment and stellar
mass divide the quenching budget, and because the measurement is reproducible
end to end: every panel in the paper is generated by a script archived beside
it.
